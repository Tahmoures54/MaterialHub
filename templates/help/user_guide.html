WHY THIS MATTERS

The project was busy. The material information was not in one place.

Picture a large construction site: the contractor is waiting, crews are ready, and a massive crane is standing idle because the next material has not arrived. Engineering has raised the requirement. Procurement is searching through spreadsheets and messages. The supplier wants to know which request is current. Warehouse has a delivery note, but connecting it to the correct PO takes time.

The problem is not always a lack of effort. It is fragmented information: scattered files, separate Excel sheets, messages, follow-up calls and knowledge that may live with one person. When that person is on leave, in another meeting or unavailable, the answer to “Where is the material?” becomes harder to find.

📁 Scattered files
📊 Separate spreadsheets
📞 Endless follow-ups
👤 Single-person dependency

MaterialHub turns that story into a visible chain: Engineering Need → MR → RFQ / PO → Delivery → Warehouse Receipt → Balance. It is designed to keep the facts available even when people are busy, moving between sites or away on leave.

Control Center

Start the working day here. Review open requisitions, purchasing activity, expected deliveries, warehouse exceptions and material risks. Do not try to inspect every record manually; use the control view to find exceptions first.

Control principle: ask “What needs attention today?” before asking “What exists in the database?”

1. Material Requisition (MR)

MR is the engineering-side statement of need. Create a clear requirement with project, item code, description / specification, quantity, unit, required date, priority and responsible discipline where applicable.

  1. Use a stable item code.
  2. Write enough specification detail for Procurement to source the correct material.
  3. Set a realistic required date.
  4. Keep quantities and units consistent.
  5. Use one coherent requirement rather than duplicating the same need across files.
Common mistake: “Ordered” is not the same as “received.” Always verify the downstream receipt.

2. RFQ & Quote Comparison

Move an approved requirement into sourcing. Invite appropriate suppliers and record commercial offers in a structured way.

  • Compare price, lead time, availability and relevant supplier performance.
  • Check whether the offered specification actually matches the requirement.
  • Record clarifications rather than keeping important decisions only in email or chat.
  • Convert the selected offer into a PO while preserving the requirement relationship.

The cheapest offer is not always the best option for the project.

3. Purchase Orders

A PO is the purchasing commitment, not proof of physical receipt. Keep supplier, quantity, price, issue date and promised delivery information accurate.

  • Update the existing PO when a commitment changes instead of creating duplicate records.
  • Watch promised dates for materials with near-term site impact.
  • Use the linked MR to understand why the PO exists.

4. Delivery

Delivery records represent logistics movement. They should be connected to the relevant PO wherever possible.

  • Record delivery identity and date.
  • Track status changes clearly.
  • Do not treat a delivery notice as the final warehouse receipt.
  • Use warehouse receipt quantity as the operational evidence of what was physically received.

5. Warehouse

Warehouse is where the digital record meets the physical material.

  1. Identify the expected delivery.
  2. Verify quantity and condition.
  3. Record the actual received quantity.
  4. Capture heat, lot, serial or batch identifiers when required.
  5. Assign the correct warehouse / location information.
  6. Keep stock status aligned with physical reality.

6. Material Reconciliation

This is the simplest control view for the question: What did Engineering need, what did we order, what was delivered, and what did the warehouse actually receive?

Signal Meaning Action
Not Ordered Requirement exists but purchasing quantity is missing or incomplete. Review procurement status.
Ordered / Awaiting Receipt Purchasing exists but warehouse has not fully received it. Check delivery commitment.
Partial Receipt / Shortage Received quantity is below requirement. Check outstanding quantity and expedite if necessary.
Surplus Received quantity is above the requirement used by the reconciliation. Verify the physical and document basis before treating it as available surplus.

Use project, material, supplier and status filters. Open the detail view when you need the underlying MR, PO, delivery and warehouse records. Open Material Reconciliation →

7. Reports & Sharing

Generate clean reports for MR, PO, Delivery and Warehouse records when a decision, approval or handover needs a clear document. Review Required, Ordered and Received numbers before sharing.

  • Use the QR code for quick access where appropriate.
  • Shared reports are read-only and use a standalone page without site navigation, footer promotions or advertising.
  • Revoke a share when it should no longer be accessible.

Open Reports →

8. QR / Barcode Traceability

For controlled materials, preserve identity across receipt, storage and issue. Capture heat, lot, serial or batch identifiers whenever applicable and keep the relationship to the material record clear.

Open Traceability →

9. Quality Documents

Keep MTC, CoC, datasheet and inspection evidence associated with the material. Make status explicit: Missing, Received, Under Review, Accepted or Rejected where the workflow supports it.

Rule: do not assume that a physically received item is automatically technically accepted.

10. Material Intelligence

Use readiness, lead-time and supplier signals to prioritize attention. These are operational signals, not automatic decisions. Validate unusual results against the source records.

Open Material Intelligence →

11. AI Material Copilot

Where enabled, Copilot should summarize evidence already present in the system and point to a concrete action. A useful response identifies the material, current requirement, open purchasing position, receipt status, required date and risk driver.

Governance: AI is decision support. Procurement, Quality and Project Management remain responsible for technical, commercial and approval decisions.

12. Recommended daily routine

  1. Morning: open Control Center and identify exceptions.
  2. Engineering: review urgent or changed requirements.
  3. Procurement: review unpurchased, late and partially supplied requirements.
  4. Delivery / Warehouse: reconcile expected deliveries with actual receipts.
  5. Project Management: review shortage and schedule-impacting items.
  6. End of day: update commitments so tomorrow’s view starts from current facts.
Core habit: MaterialHub works best when teams update the record at the moment the fact changes — not days later from memory.
