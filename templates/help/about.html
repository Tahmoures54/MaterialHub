One simple question: where is the material?

MaterialHub is built around a practical chain of control:

Engineering Need→MR→PO→Delivery→Warehouse→Balance

Instead of forcing teams to reconstruct the truth from scattered files, messages and isolated spreadsheets, MaterialHub keeps the operational trail together.

Why MaterialHub exists

There was a project site where everyone was busy… except the material.

Imagine a large construction site at the start of another demanding day.

The contractor is waiting. The crew is waiting. A huge crane is standing ready, but the next material has not arrived. Engineering has already raised the requirement, purchasing is checking old messages, the supplier is asking which revision of the request is correct, and the warehouse has a delivery note that nobody can immediately connect to the right order.

Nothing is necessarily broken. The real problem is that the information is scattered.

📁 Files in different folders
📊 Spreadsheets that drift apart
📞 Messages and follow-ups
👤 Knowledge trapped with one person

Then the questions start: How much did engineering request? How much did we order? What has actually arrived? What is short? Which PO is delayed? Is this delivery for our project? Who knows the latest status?

And when the person who normally holds all those answers is on leave, in a meeting, on another site, or simply unavailable, the process slows down again.

MaterialHub was created for exactly this moment.

It brings the material story into one place: the engineering request, purchasing, PO, delivery, warehouse receipt and material balance. It makes shortages visible. It connects facts instead of asking people to remember them. And it gives the team a shared operational view rather than another pile of files.

MaterialHub does not take lunch, go on leave, or disappear into another meeting. It keeps the material record available wherever the project team needs it—so people can spend less time searching for status and more time moving the project forward.
