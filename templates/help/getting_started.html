FIRST 30 MINUTES

Getting Started

A practical onboarding path for a new project team.

START WITH THE PROBLEM

When the crane is ready but the material is not.

On a busy project, the cost of missing information can become very real: a contractor waits, a crew is underused, a large crane stands idle, and people call one another trying to discover whether the material was requested, purchased, shipped or received.

Often the information exists—but in separate Excel files, folders, emails, messages and personal notes. If the person who knows the latest status is on leave or unavailable, the project loses time.

MaterialHub gives the team one operational trail: Need → MR → RFQ/PO → Delivery → Warehouse Receipt → Balance. The goal of this guide is to get that trail working correctly from the first day.

1. Set up the project

Before entering transactions, agree on the basic language of the project.

  • Confirm project and user roles.
  • Create supplier records used by Procurement.
  • Define warehouse and location conventions.
  • Agree on stable material/item codes and units.
  • Confirm who owns Engineering, Procurement, Quality, Delivery and Warehouse updates.
Important: consistency matters more than clever configuration. If the same pipe or valve appears under several item codes, reconciliation and reporting become harder.

2. Register material demand

Create Material Requisitions (MR) for real engineering requirements.

  1. Select the correct project.
  2. Enter item code, description/specification, quantity and unit.
  3. Set the required date and priority.
  4. Provide enough technical information for sourcing.
  5. Keep the responsible discipline/owner clear.

The required date is especially important because it allows the team to distinguish an ordinary open requirement from a time-sensitive one.

3. Run procurement

Move the requirement through RFQ and supplier comparison, then issue a Purchase Order.

RFQ

Invite appropriate suppliers and capture comparable offers.

Compare

Review price, specification fit, availability, lead time and supplier performance.

PO

Convert the selected commercial decision into a traceable purchase order.

Do not optimize only for price: a cheaper offer with an unsuitable specification or late delivery can create a larger project problem.

4. Receive and control Delivery

Keep the logistics event separate from the physical warehouse receipt.

  • Record delivery identity and expected/actual date.
  • Connect the delivery to the relevant PO.
  • Track delivery status.
  • When material physically arrives, verify quantity and condition at the warehouse.

A delivery notice says that a shipment exists; the warehouse receipt records what actually entered stock.

5. Trace and store

Record the physical truth.

  1. Verify received quantity.
  2. Record condition and acceptance state where applicable.
  3. Capture Heat/Lot/Serial/Batch identifiers for controlled materials.
  4. Assign warehouse/location information.
  5. Keep MTC, CoC, datasheet and inspection references connected where required.

6. Reconcile requirement to receipt

Open Material Reconciliation and compare the complete chain. The central question is:

What did Engineering need → what did we order → what was delivered → what did the Warehouse actually receive?

Start with the problem filter. Investigate Not Ordered, Shortage, Partial Receipt and Surplus. Open the detail view when you need the underlying MR, PO, Delivery and Warehouse records.

7. Monitor risk

Use Control Center and available material intelligence to focus attention on exceptions. Review late commitments, insufficient receipts, important required dates and supplier lead-time signals.

Signals support decisions; they do not replace the source records or the responsibility of the project team.

8. Recommended daily routine

  1. Morning: review Control Center and unresolved exceptions.
  2. Engineering: check urgent requirements and changes.
  3. Procurement: review Not Ordered, late POs and partial supply.
  4. Delivery/Warehouse: compare expected shipments with actual receipts.
  5. Quality: review outstanding technical/inspection evidence.
  6. Project Management: focus on shortages that can affect execution.
  7. End of day: update the system so the next person does not have to ask for the latest status.
The goal of the first 30 minutes is not to configure everything. It is to establish one reliable material trail that the whole team can trust.
